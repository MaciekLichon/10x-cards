Lantern Queue is a fictional build-coordination service used by a small software team. A project submits build jobs to one named queue. Each job contains a commit identifier, a target environment, and an immutable list of build steps. Lantern Queue assigns every accepted job a sequential job number within that queue. Job numbers are unique only inside their queue; two different queues may both contain job 104.

A worker may lease one waiting job at a time. A lease lasts for 90 seconds and includes a lease token. The worker must present that token when it reports progress, completes the job, or requests a lease extension. A valid extension adds 60 seconds from the moment the extension is accepted, not from the previous expiry time. The service refuses an extension submitted after the lease has expired.

Lantern Queue records a job as successful only after every listed build step reports success. If any step reports failure, the job becomes failed immediately and later step reports are ignored. A worker losing its lease does not by itself make the job fail. Instead, the job returns to the waiting state and may be leased by another worker, while reports carrying the old lease token are rejected.

Projects can attach one notification channel to a queue. The service sends a notification when a job first becomes successful or failed. Returning an expired job to the waiting state does not send a notification. Repeated completion reports do not create repeated notifications, even if they carry the valid token used for the original completion.

The service retains the final status, step results, and notification outcome for 14 days after completion. It then deletes those records together. The submitted commit identifier and target environment are not copied into a separate long-term archive. Deleting a queue before the retention period ends removes its completed and waiting jobs immediately, but it does not delete another queue even when both queues use the same display name.
